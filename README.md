# IBM HR Attrition & Performance Analysis Dashboard

### An Interactive Excel HR Analytics Dashboard

<p>
This project presents an interactive <b>IBM HR Attrition & Performance Analysis Dashboard</b> developed in Microsoft Excel. 
The dashboard is designed to analyze employee workforce data, understand employee attrition patterns, and provide useful HR insights through KPIs, Pivot Tables, Pivot Charts, slicers, and interactive visualizations.
</p>

</div>

<hr>

<h2>📌 Project Overview</h2>

<p>
Employee attrition is one of the most important challenges faced by Human Resource departments. 
Understanding why employees leave an organization can help management improve employee satisfaction, retention strategies, workforce planning, and overall organizational performance.
</p>

<p>
In this project, I analyzed IBM HR employee data and created an interactive Excel dashboard that summarizes important workforce and attrition-related information in a simple and visually appealing format.
</p>

<p>
The dashboard allows users to analyze employees by department, age group, job role, job level, gender, marital status, job satisfaction, employee status, and attrition.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>🎯 Project Objectives</h2>

<ul>
    <li>Analyze the overall employee workforce.</li>
    <li>Measure the total number of employees who left the organization.</li>
    <li>Calculate the employee attrition rate.</li>
    <li>Analyze the average number of years employees stay at the company.</li>

    <li>Analyze employee job satisfaction levels.</li>

<h2>📈 Dashboard Analysis</h2>

<h3>1. Employee Status</h3>

<p>
The Employee Status doughnut chart compares active employees with employees who have left the organization.
Out of 320 total employees, 64 employees have left the company, while the remaining employees are still active.
</p>

<p>
This provides a quick overview of workforce retention and helps HR understand the proportion of employees experiencing attrition.
</p>

<h3>2. Department-wise Employees</h3>

<p>
The Department-wise Employees chart shows the number of employees working in different departments, including:
</p>

<ul>
    <li>Human Resources</li>
    <li>Research & Development</li>
    <li>Sales</li>
</ul>

<p>
The analysis shows that <b>Research & Development</b> contains the largest number of employees, followed by Sales, while Human Resources has the smallest workforce.
</p>

<h3>3. Age Group Distribution</h3>

<p>
Employees have been divided into different age groups to understand workforce demographics:
</p>

<ul>
    <li>Under 25</li>
    <li>25–34</li>
    <li>35–44</li>

The dashboard indicates that the largest employee concentration is within the <b>35–44 age group</b>.

<h2>📐 Important Calculations</h2>

<h3>Attrition Rate</h3>

<p>
The Attrition Rate was calculated using the following formula:
</p>

<pre>
Attrition Rate = (Left Employees / Total Employees) × 100
</pre>

<p>For this dataset:</p>

<pre>
Attrition Rate = (64 / 320) × 100
               = 20%
</pre>

<p>
Therefore, approximately <b>20% of employees</b> in the analyzed dataset have left the organization.
</p>

    <li>The 35–44 age group represents one of the largest employee groups.</li>
    <li>Research & Development and Sales contribute significantly to overall employee attrition.</li>
    <li>Employee job satisfaction should be monitored alongside attrition to identify possible retention issues.</li>
    <li>Job role distribution shows that workforce size varies considerably across different organizational roles.</li>
</ul>

<h2>💡 Business Recommendations</h2>

<p>
Based on the dashboard analysis, HR management can focus on several areas to improve employee retention.
Departments showing higher attrition should be analyzed in greater detail to understand the reasons employees leave.
</p>

<p>
Management should also monitor employee job satisfaction, career growth opportunities, compensation, workload, work-life balance, and employee engagement.
</p>

<p>
Age groups and job roles experiencing higher attrition can also be studied separately so that targeted retention strategies can be developed.
</p>

<p>
Regular HR dashboard monitoring can help management identify workforce problems earlier and support more data-driven HR decisions.
